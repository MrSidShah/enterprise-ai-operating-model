# AI Activation Across a Federated International Media Organisation

## Context

A large international media organisation operating across 50+ markets with distinct editorial, commercial, and technology teams in each region. Centralised technology investment existed but AI adoption was fragmented — individual markets running disconnected experiments, duplicating tooling, and producing inconsistent outcomes.

No employer or organisation is named in this document. The patterns described are transferable across any large, federated, multi-market business.

---

## The Challenge

- No shared AI infrastructure — each market sourcing its own tools
- Limited data interoperability between markets and business units
- Editorial and commercial teams operating with entirely different AI priorities
- No governance framework — compliance and brand risk from uncontrolled AI outputs
- Executive pressure to "do AI" without a clear commercial mandate

---

## The Approach

### Phase 1 — Establish the Foundation

Rather than deploying AI use cases immediately, the programme began with three foundational investments:

- A unified audience data model across markets, enabling consistent signals for AI systems
- A shared AI platform — one evaluation framework, one observability layer, one set of approved model providers
- A federated governance model — central standards, local execution accountability

This phase was deliberately invisible to end users. Its purpose was to make everything that followed faster and safer.

### Phase 2 — Prioritise for Commercial Impact

Use cases were prioritised using a simple two-axis framework:

- **Commercial value potential** (revenue impact, cost reduction, margin improvement)
- **Data and technical readiness** (quality of underlying data, integration complexity)

The highest-priority use cases were not always the most technically interesting — they were the ones where data was clean, integration was straightforward, and commercial measurement was unambiguous.

### Phase 3 — Deploy, Measure, Scale

Initial deployments targeted three areas:

**Audience personalisation** — AI-driven content recommendations across digital properties, measured against engagement and subscription conversion rates.

**Commercial optimisation** — Pricing and inventory recommendations for advertising, measured against yield improvement.

**Operational acceleration** — Workflow automation for content metadata, tagging, and distribution, measured against time-to-publish reduction.

Each deployment included a pre-agreed commercial KPI, an evaluation framework, and an observability layer before going live.

---

## What Worked

**Shared infrastructure reduced time-to-deployment significantly.**
Markets building on a shared platform moved from concept to production in weeks rather than months. The investment in foundation paid back quickly.

**Federated governance created accountability without blocking innovation.**
Central standards set the floor — model evaluation, data governance, compliance requirements. Markets retained freedom above that floor. The model scaled without requiring central approval for every deployment.

**Commercial KPIs changed the conversation with leadership.**
Framing AI programmes around revenue impact and margin improvement, rather than capability deployment, sustained executive sponsorship through the inevitable setbacks of early production deployments.

---

## What Didn't Work

**Market maturity variation created uneven progress.**
Markets with stronger data infrastructure moved faster and delivered better outcomes. The programme underestimated the time required to bring lower-maturity markets to a foundation level. A tiered rollout model would have been more effective than a uniform timeline.

**Organisational change was underinvested relative to technology.**
The technology deployed faster than teams could absorb it. Adoption required dedicated enablement — not just tools, but workflow redesign, training, and change management. This was scoped too lightly in the initial plan.

---

## Key Transferable Patterns

| Pattern | Application |
|---|---|
| Foundation before use cases | Invest in data, governance, and shared infrastructure before deploying AI at scale |
| Commercial KPI first | Define the business metric before selecting the AI approach |
| Federated accountability | Central standards, local execution — not central control |
| Tiered rollout by maturity | Sequence deployment based on data and organisational readiness |
| Observability from day one | Instrument before deploying, not after the first incident |

---

## Relevance

This pattern applies to any large organisation with:
- Multiple business units or geographies operating semi-independently
- Pressure to scale AI beyond isolated pilots
- Mixed data maturity across the organisation
- A need to balance central governance with local innovation
