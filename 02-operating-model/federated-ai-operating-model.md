# Federated AI Operating Model

## Overview

A federated AI operating model balances central governance and shared platform capabilities with distributed domain execution across markets, business units and functions.

This approach is increasingly necessary in large organisations where:
- business units operate independently
- regional requirements vary
- data ecosystems differ across markets
- AI adoption must scale without central bottlenecks

---

# Core Operating Principles

## Central Enablement

Central teams provide:
- enterprise AI platforms
- governance standards
- reusable orchestration frameworks
- evaluation tooling
- security controls
- observability standards
- shared infrastructure

## Distributed Domain Execution

Domain and business teams retain responsibility for:
- business workflow integration
- operational implementation
- local experimentation
- adoption and enablement
- market-specific optimisation

# Common Federated AI Challenges

## Platform Fragmentation

Without shared standards:
- duplicated tooling emerges
- governance weakens
- operational costs increase
- technical debt expands

## Centralised Delivery Bottlenecks

Over-centralised AI teams can:
- slow deployment
- reduce innovation
- weaken domain alignment
- create operational dependency

## Governance Inconsistency

Without shared governance:
- model quality varies
- risks increase
- compliance gaps emerge
- trust declines

# In Practice — What Actually Works

**The balance point is never static.**
In a federated organisation spanning multiple markets or business units, what works in a mature market with strong data infrastructure will not transfer directly to an emerging one. The central vs. local balance needs to flex by market maturity, not be imposed uniformly from the centre.

**Governance without trust fails.**
The most common federated AI breakdown is central teams imposing governance frameworks that domain teams experience as blockers rather than enablers. The frameworks that stick are the ones domain teams helped design — even partially. Buy-in from the edges is not optional.

**Shared platforms only work if adoption is earned.**
Central AI platforms succeed when they demonstrably reduce domain teams' time to delivery. If the central platform requires more effort than building locally, teams will build locally — every time. Platform value must be visible and fast, not theoretical and promised.
